    Workload Portfolio

    SIM-01 // PORTFOLIO

    GPU-years consumed per domain, compute bottlenecks, and fleet composition. The scaling-case fan shows how uncertain scaling laws can swing your fleet requirement.

    The domain stack tells you where your GPU-years go. Look for concentration risk — if LLM dominates, your fleet is memory-bandwidth-limited and benefits from B200/H200. The bottleneck heatmap reveals whether you need more HBM, FP4 compute, NVLink bandwidth, or FP64 cores. The scaling-case fan shows how much your fleet could grow if AI-for-science turns out to be compute-bound.

    Chip Mix & Spend

    SIM-02 // FINANCIAL

    Fleet composition over the horizon, cumulative TCO breakdown, and the crossover point where building beats renting.

    The build-vs-buy crossover is the most important chart here. If your internal cost/Mtok never crosses below the declining external rental curve, you may be better off staying on cloud. The TCO stack shows whether CapEx (GPUs, networking, facility) or OpEx (power, rental) dominates — this drives your depreciation and refresh strategy.

    Latency & Data Center

    SIM-03 // INFRASTRUCTURE

    Power footprint, facility count by latency tier, scale-up fabric sizing, and the latency-cost Pareto frontier per Erdil/Epoch.

    The Pareto frontier shows you the cheapest cost-per-token achievable at each latency target. Moving left (faster) is expensive — interactive serving at 30ms/tok can cost 3–10× more than batch at 500ms/tok. If the frontier model requires more coherent-fabric GPUs than your chosen SKU provides, you'll need pipeline-parallel splits or a different chip.

    Long-Term & Surge

    SIM-04 // UNCERTAINTY

    Demand uncertainty fan vs provisioned capacity, and TCO sensitivity to each input lever.

    The tornado chart tells you which assumptions matter most. Widen the top bars to pressure-test your plan. The demand fan shows how much cushion your provisioned capacity provides — if the P90 demand line exceeds capacity in years 3–5, you need either more headroom or a cloud-overflow strategy. Demand growth rate and algorithmic efficiency gains are usually the widest bars.

All numbers are illustrative; tune parameters to your situation.
